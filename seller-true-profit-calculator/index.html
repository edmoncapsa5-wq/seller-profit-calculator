    How this works

    How is profit calculated?

    Selling price minus percentage-based deductions minus product cost and all other per-order costs. Percentage deductions are assumed to apply to the selling price.

    What do the percentage results mean?

    Profit margin is profit divided by selling price. Net return on product cost is actual profit divided by product cost. The second figure is not traditional markup; it shows how much net profit remains relative to the item cost after all entered deductions.

    Why isn't break-even simply cost + fees?

    Percentage fees rise when price rises. Break-even therefore uses fixed costs ÷ (1 − total percentage rate). Target-margin prices use fixed costs ÷ (1 − percentage rate − target margin).

    Calculation assumption: For consistency, percentage deductions are applied to the entered selling price, while seller-funded discounts are treated as a separate per-order deduction. Your platform may use a different fee base; check your order statement.

    Estimate only. Marketplace fees, taxes, shipping arrangements, refunds, returns and program-specific charges vary. Enter your actual applicable figures and verify them against platform statements before making pricing decisions.
